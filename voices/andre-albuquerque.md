# André Albuquerque: LinkedIn voice

Style only. Public. Used by `/ship-story` when the author is `andre-albuquerque`.

## Precedence

- This file wins for his personal LinkedIn posts, including opening with "Here's", which a project's anti-AI voice rules may otherwise ban on social.
- For everything else (guides, site copy, emails, page text), the project's copy rules apply.
- No em dashes and no en dashes, ever, in anything written for him. He does not use them.
- No hashtags.

## Stance

- Personal proof: his own businesses, his own schedule, what he actually did.
- Direct and opinionated. A contrarian or bold claim is fine, often addressed to the reader.
- Concrete numbers: 4,000 people, 90%, €50M in 18 months, 0.5% → 200 people per sale.
- Never corporate filler.
- Keep his casual phrasing as a style signal. Do not introduce new typos.

## Sentence rhythm

- One idea per line, lots of white space.
- Numbered lists or arrow lists (→, >, *).
- Short Q&A beats: "Do I care? No."
- Short closers: "It's up to you.", "Hope this helps.", "Sounds like a win win."

## Vocabulary

- Plain, spoken English. Words like "playbook", "insane", "cracked", "dump your notes".
- Single words in CAPS for stress (OUTDATED, MANDATORY, LEVEL 10).
- Tools named plainly: Claude, Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT.
- Company pages he tags: Builders Camp, Super Operator AI, Producthon.

## Hooks

- One bold claim or contrarian line, often addressed to the reader.
- "Here's ..." openers are allowed and common.
- A setup line that promises a list ("here's my playbook 👇", "Here's what each person said 👇").

## Emphasis

- Exactly one Unicode-bold punchline sentence per post (𝐥𝐢𝐤𝐞 𝐭𝐡𝐢𝐬).
- Unicode-italic asides in brackets (𝘭𝘪𝘬𝘦 𝘵𝘩𝘪𝘴).
- CAPS on single words.

## Endings

- A question to readers ("Which role did I miss? Anyone disagree?"), and/or
- a PS pointing to BuildersCamp.com.
- Or a short closer ("Hope this helps.").

## Hard no's

- Em dashes and en dashes (also in number ranges).
- Hashtags.
- Corporate filler.
- New typos.

### Post 2

If you want to build products, become a solopreneur, launch your side-business, BUT don't know how to get started, here's my playbook  👇 

  1.  find someone with a problem (𝘪𝘵'𝘴 𝘧𝘪𝘯𝘦 𝘪𝘧 𝘪𝘵'𝘴 𝘺𝘰𝘶)
  2.  talk to that person about it, go as deep as you can
  3.  Find a second person and talk to them. See where both are similar
  4.  Dump your conversation notes to Claude or ChatGPT
  5.  Ask what are the learnings, common issues, similar pains
  6.  Start thinking what is a product or service (𝘰𝘳 𝘣𝘰𝘵𝘩) that could solve for those pains
  7.  Ask where's the defensibility of the ideas, what could be valuable
  8.  Ask for a PRD optimized for AI to build the product. Ask it to be technical
  9.  Open Claude Code or Codex and paste the PRD. Put Auto-Mode. Answer questions. Run it until you get an MVP in a working URL
  10. Bring the MVP to those two people and get them testing
  11. Ask for feedback, see what's working and failing. Ask if they would pay for it
  12. Dump the notes on Claude or Chatgpt again, and ask for a follow up
  13. Aggregate all in a new PRD and give it again to Claude Code or Codex
  14. Repeat every step until one of those people offers to pay
  15. Now, keep it going until you get more people paying

Anyone can do these fifteen steps.
It's up to you.

If going to bed on Sunday sucks, it's time to start.

 * Growth

 * Lead Gen

 * Generating awareness

𝐓𝐡𝐢𝐬 𝐢𝐬 𝐭𝐡𝐞 𝐧𝐞𝐰 "𝐮𝐧𝐟𝐚𝐢𝐫 𝐚𝐝𝐯𝐚𝐧𝐭𝐚𝐠𝐞" 𝐨𝐟 𝐩𝐫𝐨𝐝𝐮𝐜𝐭 𝐛𝐮𝐢𝐥𝐝𝐞𝐫𝐬.

Knowing how to build something, actually working well with agentic development, is just LEVEL 1.

Mastering Go-to-Market will feel like LEVEL 10.

Here's my quick playbook for GTM if you're building your own products (it's what I did to grow Builders Camp, Super Operator AI and Producthon):

  > Try different free channels first (LinkedIn, Twitter, Reddit)
  > Create content yourself, it's "free" and works better than ads
  > Get feedback on your landing page copy. Everyone needs to say "yeah, makes absolute sense"
  > Do something every day. Every day. If you can, do two different things a day.
  > Ask every user for a referral. Just one person. They will be the most likely to convert.
  > Know your funnel and fill the top. If you convert 0.5%, you need 200 people per sale. Find many groups of 200 people.
  > Build a pipeline of experiments. Many ideas per channel. Try them all, even if it sounds dumb.

There is no way to know what will work. 𝐓𝐫𝐲𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐦𝐨𝐫𝐞 𝐆𝐓𝐌 𝐭𝐚𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐜𝐬 𝐢𝐬 𝐨𝐟𝐭𝐞𝐧 𝐛𝐞𝐭𝐭𝐞𝐫 𝐭𝐡𝐚𝐧 𝐚𝐝𝐝𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐧𝐢𝐜𝐞-𝐭𝐨-𝐡𝐚𝐯𝐞 𝐟𝐞𝐚𝐭𝐮𝐫𝐞𝐬.

### Post 6

I deeply disagree with this, whether you're doing VC-backed or bootstrapped. It's all about the long game. Why?

𝐁𝐞𝐜𝐚𝐮𝐬𝐞 𝐭𝐡𝐢𝐧𝐠𝐬 𝐭𝐚𝐤𝐞 𝐰𝐚𝐲 𝐥𝐨𝐧𝐠𝐞𝐫 𝐭𝐡𝐚𝐧 𝐢𝐭 𝐬𝐞𝐞𝐦𝐬. Way longer (this is one of the main topics when mentoring at Builders Camp btw)

If you're not playing the long game, you'll burn your mind before you get there.

I'm running 6 business lines in my holding company (all revenue-generating and profitable) and I've designed my reality to be my own long game:

  *   amazing family and a 2 year old
  *   gym every day, start work at 10am
  *   leave work at 4:30pm to pick kid from school
  *   end of day is always playtime, no work distractions
  *   surf once or twice per week, during mornings
  *   rarely work nights (maybe twice a month)
  *   most weekends away from home, no work
  *   no specific business targets or KPIs
  *   barely no team (one person only)
  *   ~zero time wasted on meetings or useless stuff
  *   go to bed on Sunday feeling pumped
  *   wake up on Monday feeling pumped

Could my business be 5x larger? Yeah probably.

Do I care? No.

It's profitable for 4 years.
I've distributed 6 figures of dividends every year.

Is it going to be a unicorn or be sold for tens of millions? No.

Do I care? Definitely not.

You should optimize for what feels the best life possible, not what others think.
